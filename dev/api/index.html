API reference

GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.Q_COP — Method
Q_COP(Q, COP_heating, COP_cooling)

Convert building thermal load(s) to ground thermal load(s) based on heat pump COP. Inputs can be scalar or vector values with the following sign convention:

  • Qb > 0: building heating load -> Qg < 0: heat extracted from the ground loop
  • Qb < 0: building cooling load -> Qg > 0: heat rejected to the ground loop

For heating, the conversion is Qg = -Qb * (1 - 1 / COP_heating). For cooling, the conversion is Qg = -Qb * (1 + 1 / COP_cooling).

Arguments

- `Qb`: Building thermal load, scalar or vector [W]
- `COP_heating`: Heating COP, scalar or vector
- `COP_cooling`: Cooling COP, scalar or vector

Output

- `Qg`: Ground thermal load, scalar or vector with the same shape as `Qb` [W]

GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign._stationary_ground_temperature — Method
_stationary_ground_temperature(t, Qg, xy, rb, H, D, ks, Cs, s, ro, ri, kg, kp, kf, cf, ρf, μf,
    V, T0)

Ground temperature entering (T_in) and leaving (T_out) a borehole field for a constant flow rate V, by stationary (FFT) convolution of the ground load Qg with the field's g-function. See the README's "Conventions" section for the T_in/T_out nomenclature. Shared by every gshp_simulation method — internal helper, not exported.

GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.annual_drift — Method
annual_drift(T, steps_per_year)

Difference between the mean temperature of the last simulated year and the first, an indicator of the long-term thermal drift of the ground.

Arguments

- `T`: Temperature time series [°C], covering an integer number of years
- `steps_per_year`: Number of time steps per year (e.g. `8760` for an hourly simulation)

Output

- Annual drift, last year's mean minus first year's mean [°C]

GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.ground_load_capped — Method
ground_load_capped(Qb, COP_heating, COP_cooling, CAP_heating_total, CAP_cooling_total)

Convert a building thermal load to a ground thermal load using a fixed nominal COP (rather than heat_pump_performance's source-EFT-dependent COP), after clamping the building load to what the installed heat pumps can actually deliver. This is the direct, non-iterative counterpart to ground_load_from_heat_pump: since the source EFT is not yet known before the ground temperature is simulated, the COP here is a fixed design value instead of being looked up at the (unknown) operating temperature, so no outer iteration on the ground response is needed. Any load beyond CAP_heating_total/CAP_cooling_total is reported as unmet rather than covered.

Arguments

- `Qb`: Building thermal load, scalar or vector [W]
- `COP_heating`: Nominal heating COP used for the conversion (fixed, not EFT-dependent) [-]
- `COP_cooling`: Nominal cooling COP used for the conversion (fixed, not EFT-dependent) [-]
- `CAP_heating_total`: Combined heating capacity of the installed heat pumps, e.g.
    `n_HP * maximum(CAP_heat_vec)` [W]
- `CAP_cooling_total`: Combined cooling capacity of the installed heat pumps [W]

Output

- `Qg`: Ground thermal load, same sign convention as [`Q_COP`](@ref) [W]
- `Qb_unmet`: Building load left unmet by the installed heat pump capacity, same shape as `Qb`
    [W, or kW]
source
GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.ground_load_from_heat_pump — Method
ground_load_from_heat_pump(Q_building, Tsrc, Tload, perf_heating, perf_cooling)

Convert a building thermal load to the corresponding ground load, accounting for the heat pump's capacity limit at the current source/load entering fluid temperatures (EFTs). The heat pump's CAP and COP are looked up from perf_heating (if Q_building > 0, heating) or perf_cooling (if Q_building < 0, cooling) via heat_pump_performance. Each is a tuple of arguments forwarded to that function (either (Tsrc_grid, CAP_vec, COP_vec) for the 1D overload or (Tsrc_grid, Tload_grid, CAP_table, COP_table) for the 2D overload). The heat pump's contribution is capped at its capacity (any load beyond that is reported as unmet rather than extrapolated).

Arguments

- `Q_building`: Building thermal load, `> 0` for heating, `< 0` for cooling [W]
- `Tsrc`: Source entering fluid temperature (EFT) [°C]
- `Tload`: Load entering fluid temperature (EFT) [°C]
- `perf_heating`: Heat pump heating performance data, given to [`heat_pump_performance`](@ref)
- `perf_cooling`: Heat pump cooling performance data, given to [`heat_pump_performance`](@ref)

Output

- `Q_ground`: Ground load using the same sign convention as [`Q_COP`](@ref) [W, or kW]
- `Q_unmet`: Building load left unmet because it exceeds the heat pump's capacity [W, or kW]
source
GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.gshp_simulation — Method
gshp_simulation(t, Qb, perf_heating, perf_cooling, n_HP,
    xy, rb, H, D, ks, Cs, s, ro, ri, kg, kp, kf, cf, ρf, μf, V, T0)

Case 2: building load to ground load with temperature-dependent COP/CAP (heat_pump_performance curves), a stationary (constant flow rate) ground response, and no source-side load shaving. The building-to-ground conversion still can't use the curve directly (the source EFT isn't known until the ground response has been simulated), so it uses the mean of the curve as a fixed nominal COP for that one pass (ground_load_capped); once the resulting T_out is known, n_active_heat_pumps reports the number of units operating and the COP actually achieved — a diagnostic, not fed back into the simulation. See the README's "Conventions" section for the sign convention and T_in/T_out nomenclature.

Arguments

- `t`: Time vector [s], uniformly spaced, matching `Qb`
- `Qb`: Building thermal load [W], `> 0` heating, `< 0` cooling, same length as `t`
- `perf_heating`, `perf_cooling`: Single heat pump's heating/cooling performance data,
    forwarded to [`heat_pump_performance`](@ref) (`(Tsrc_grid, CAP_vec, COP_vec)`)
- `n_HP`: Number of installed heat pumps
- remaining arguments: as in the fixed-COP [`gshp_simulation`](@ref) method

Output

Named tuple with fields:
- `Qg`: Ground thermal load [W]
- `Qb_hp`: Building load actually delivered by the heat pumps [W]
- `Qb_unmet`: Building load left unmet (auxiliary) [W]
- `T_in`, `T_out`: Ground temperature entering/leaving the field [°C]
- `n_active`: Fractional number of heat pumps operating, in `[0, n_HP]`
- `COP_actual`: COP actually achieved at `T_out`, from the same interpolation as `n_active`
- `Rbe`: Effective borehole thermal resistance [m.K/W]
- `g`: g-function used for the ground response [°C.m/W]

Reference

- Pasquier, P., Marcotte, D., Bernier, M., & Kummert, M. (2013). Simulation of ground-coupled
    heat pump systems using a spectral approach. Proceedings of BS2013, 2691–2698.

GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.gshp_simulation — Method
gshp_simulation(t, Qb, COP_heating, COP_cooling, CAP_heating_unit, CAP_cooling_unit, n_HP,
    xy, rb, H, D, ks, Cs, s, ro, ri, kg, kp, kf, cf, ρf, μf, V, T0)

Case 1, the simplest GSHP simulation: building load to ground load with fixed (temperature-independent) COP and CAP, a stationary (constant flow rate) ground response, and no source-side load shaving — any load beyond the installed capacity is simply unmet. See ground_load_capped for the load conversion step and _stationary_ground_temperature for the ground response, and the README's "Conventions" section for the sign convention and T_in/T_out nomenclature.

Arguments

- `t`: Time vector [s], uniformly spaced, matching `Qb`
- `Qb`: Building thermal load [W], `> 0` heating, `< 0` cooling, same length as `t`
- `COP_heating`, `COP_cooling`: Fixed nominal heating/cooling COP of one heat pump unit
- `CAP_heating_unit`, `CAP_cooling_unit`: Fixed heating/cooling capacity of one heat pump unit
    [W]
- `n_HP`: Number of installed heat pumps
- `xy`: Borehole coordinates (nb x 2) [m]
- `rb`: Borehole radius [m]
- `H`, `D`: Borehole depth and buried depth [m]
- `ks`, `Cs`: Ground thermal conductivity [W/mK] and volumetric heat capacity [J/m3K]
- `s`: Shank spacing [m]
- `ro`, `ri`: Pipe outer/inner radius [m]
- `kg`, `kp`: Grout and pipe thermal conductivity [W/mK]
- `kf`, `cf`, `ρf`, `μf`: Fluid thermal conductivity [W/mK], specific heat [J/kgK], density
    [kg/m3] and dynamic viscosity [kg/(m.s)]
- `V`: Flow rate per borehole [m3/s]
- `T0`: Undisturbed ground temperature [°C]

Output

Named tuple with fields:
- `Qg`: Ground thermal load [W]
- `Qb_hp`: Building load actually delivered by the heat pumps [W]
- `Qb_unmet`: Building load left unmet (auxiliary) [W]
- `T_in`, `T_out`: Ground temperature entering/leaving the field [°C]
- `n_active`: Fractional number of heat pumps operating, in `[0, n_HP]`
- `Rbe`: Effective borehole thermal resistance [m.K/W]
- `g`: g-function used for the ground response [°C.m/W]

GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.heat_pump_performance — Method
heat_pump_performance(Tsrc, Tsrc_grid, CAP_vec, COP_vec)
heat_pump_performance(Tsrc, Tload, Tsrc_grid, Tload_grid, CAP_table, COP_table)

Function that calculates heat pump capacity (CAP) and coefficient of performance (COP) based on the source and load entering temperatures. The function has two overloads:

  • 1D overload: linear interpolation on source entering fluid temperature (EFT). In this case, the load entering temperature is not considered and the provided CAP and COP values are only a function of the source EFT.
  • 2D overload: bilinear interpolation on source and load entering temperatures. In this case, the provided CAP and COP values are a function of both source and load EFTs.

For both overloads, values are clamped outside the provided grid bounds.

Arguments

- `Tsrc`: Source entering fluid temperature (EFT, or EWT) (Tout of the ground loop) [°C]
- `Tload`: Load entering fluid temperature (EFT) (Tout of the building loop) [°C]
- `Tsrc_grid`: Grid of source EFT values corresponding to the provided CAP and COP values [°C]
- `Tload_grid`: Grid of load EFT values corresponding to the provided CAP and COP values [°C]
- `CAP_vec`: Vector of heat pump CAPs corresponding to the source EFT grid (1D) [W]
- `COP_vec`: Vector of heat pump COPs corresponding to the source EFT grid (1D) [W]
- `CAP_table`: Matrix of heat pump CAPs corresponding to the source and load EFT grids (2D) [W]
- `COP_table`: Matrix of heat pump COPs corresponding to the source and load EFT grids (2D) [W]

Output

- Heat pump CAP and coefficient of performance (COP) corresponding to the provided
    source and load EFTs.
source
GroundSourceHeatPumpDesign.n_active_heat_pumps — Method
n_active_heat_pumps(Qb_capped, Tsrc, n_HP, perf_heating, perf_cooling)

Number of heat pumps operating at each time step to deliver Qb_capped, given the source EFT Tsrc (e.g. from a ground temperature simulation) and a single unit's performance curve. Following Pasquier et al. (2013), this is a fractional, time-averaged count (between 0 and n_HP) rather than an integer, since a heat pump typically cycles within a time step instead of switching exactly on its boundary.

Arguments

- `Qb_capped`: Building load actually delivered by the heat pumps (post-capacity-capping, e.g.
    from [`ground_load_capped`](@ref)) [W]
- `Tsrc`: Source entering fluid temperature (EFT) [°C]
- `n_HP`: Number of installed heat pumps
- `perf_heating`: Single heat pump's heating performance data, forwarded to
    [`heat_pump_performance`](@ref) (`(Tsrc_grid, CAP_vec, COP_vec)`)
- `perf_cooling`: Single heat pump's cooling performance data, forwarded to
    [`heat_pump_performance`](@ref)

Output

- `n`: Fractional number of heat pumps operating, in `[0, n_HP]`
- `COP`: Coefficient of performance at each time step, from the same interpolation as `n`

Reference

- Pasquier, P., Marcotte, D., Bernier, M., & Kummert, M. (2013). Simulation of ground-coupled
    heat pump systems using a spectral approach. Proceedings of BS2013, 2691–2698.
source

Total thermal energy of a load time series, by rectangular (constant time step) integration.

Arguments

- `Q`: Thermal load time series [W, or kW]
- `dt`: Time step duration [h]

Output

- Total energy, in the same power unit times hours (e.g. kWh if `Q` is in kW)
